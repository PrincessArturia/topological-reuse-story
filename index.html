Topological reuse · research in progress

Networks that solve similar problems build similar shapes. One network can read those shapes and reuse its own circuits.

One idea, tested in numbered notebooks: networks that solve structurally similar problems build similar circuit shapes (rings and waves on clock arithmetic), so one network can read those shapes from a short probe and reuse its own circuits through small lenses, learning faster, smaller and from less data.

1 · The shape

A ring says “clock”. Its arrangement says which clock.

A small network learned to add on a 31-hour clock. What it built is nine rings, each with the thirty-one numbers around it, each counting its way around at a different rate. The network found this on its own; it was never shown a clock.

The rings can be found without being told where to look: groups of neurons that read the same directions give them away (notebook 3). And they can be put to other use: a lens of 64 to 80 trained numbers turns the frozen rings of addition into subtraction, and taking the lens off restores addition (notebook 5).

3 · The loop

What the reader sees, what deciding costs, what an adapter weighs

Before it can reuse a circuit, the network has to decide which one. It trains a short probe on the new task and reads the probe's shape: deciding by shape from a 500-step probe of the output end is accurate, cheap and robust to unseen formulas (notebooks 7, 8 and 11).

Run as one loop over a stream of 15 tasks, a single network makes 12 of 13 right calls in every seed and ends at 59–64% of the size of separate networks, with 24–36% of the steps (notebook 8).

What stands

  • Shape carries reuse: the ring lens works only on real rings (nb 5, 9).
  • Reuse goes through lenses on frozen circuits; freezing is what makes 96-pair learning possible (nb 5, 9).
  • Deciding by shape from a 500-step probe of the output end is accurate, cheap and robust to unseen formulas (nb 7, 8, 11).
  • One network runs the loop with the three savings: space, steps, data (nb 6, 8).
  • Against a fair shared network, a frozen circuit with a table tune still learns a new task in fewer steps (5/5 seeds) and is ahead with 96 pairs in every seed, by 23-42 points, short of the 25-point bar in two (nb 10b).
